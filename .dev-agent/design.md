# Design: let go on a small scroll up

**One file changes:** `frontend/src/hooks/use-reader-scrolled-up.ts`. Nothing else in `src/` moves (not `useFollowContent`, `chat-container.tsx` or `turn-parts.tsx`).

1. **The at-foot re-arm needs a scroll that did not move up.** Line 43 becomes `if (fromFoot <= AT_FOOT_PX && top >= lastTop)`. Why: a desktop wheel scroll is animated, so after `onWheel` lets go, the first frames land 2-20px from the foot. Today line 43 re-arms on any of them, whatever the direction, and the next growth pins the reader back down. A scroll that moved up near the foot is the reader leaving, so it no longer re-arms. A scroll down to the foot, or a scroll event with no movement (`top === lastTop`), still re-arms.
2. **A grab of the scroller's own scrollbar is intent.** Add a local `let grabbing = false`. A `pointerdown` listener on `element` sets `grabbing = event.target === element`. The content sits inside the scroller, so a press whose target is the scroller itself is a press on its scrollbar (or its bare padding, which is harmless: the only scroll that press can drive is the reader's own). Assigning, not only setting, means a press on content (a text-selection drag) is never a grab and also clears a stale one. `pointerup` and `pointercancel` set `grabbing = false`, and they listen on **`window` in the capture phase**. The reason: a drag usually ends outside the scroller's box. A mouse has no implicit pointer capture, and browsers do not agree on capture for native scrollbars, so a listener on `element` misses a release outside the box. On `window` it hears a release anywhere in the document, and in the capture phase no content handler that calls `stopPropagation` can swallow it. The let-go branch (line 44) becomes `else if (top < lastTop && (grabbing || fromFoot > LET_GO_PX)) letGo();`. While a grab is held, any scroll up lets go, whatever its distance from the foot.
3. **Everything else stays as it is.** `onWheel`, the touch handlers, `onKey` and `UP_KEYS` do not change. `LET_GO_PX` still decides for a scroll up with no gesture behind it, for example scroll anchoring after the content above shrinks. Its doc comment (line 5) and the hook's doc (lines 20-22) get updated to say this.

**It fails safe.** If a browser fires no `pointerdown` for a scrollbar grab (unverified in Firefox), `grabbing` never becomes true and line 44 is today's rule exactly. Change 1 alone still fixes the wheel case. A missed release leaves `grabbing` stuck only until the next press or release anywhere in the page. Meanwhile the worst case is that a gesture-less scroll up under 150px lets go, so the reader is left in place rather than pulled down.

**Teardown:** the effect's cleanup also removes `pointerdown` from `element`, and `pointerup` and `pointercancel` from `window` with the same `true` capture flag. Without that flag the removal does not match.

**Tests:** extend `frontend/src/hooks/use-reader-scrolled-up.test.tsx` in its own style: Testing Library `render` plus `fireEvent`, and geometry faked through the existing `at()` helper (`Object.defineProperty` on `scrollHeight`, `clientHeight` and `scrollTop`, then `fireEvent.scroll`). The `Scroller` helper gains one child element so a press on content can be told apart from a press on the scroller. jsdom 30 has `PointerEvent`, so `fireEvent.pointerDown`, `pointerUp` and `pointerCancel` work, and a release fired on `document.body` reaches the `window` capture listener. `src/hooks/**` is under the gate (`frontend/vitest.config.ts` line 58: 100% lines, statements and functions, 97.5% branches).

**CHANGELOG** (a new `### Fixed` under `## [Unreleased]`):

- **A small scroll up while an answer streams stays where the reader put it.** A turn of the mouse wheel, or a short drag of the scrollbar, that stayed near the bottom of the chat was undone by the answer's next line, which pulled the view back down. Only a large scroll got away. Now any scroll up by wheel or scrollbar leaves the view where the reader moved it, and scrolling back to the bottom follows the answer again.
